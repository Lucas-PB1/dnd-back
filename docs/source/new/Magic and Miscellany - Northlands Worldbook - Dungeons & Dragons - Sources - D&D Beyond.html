Chapter 5: Magic and Miscellany

In the Northlands, surviving the harsh world of ice and snow requires bravery and strength but also proper preparation. The right gear can mean the difference between life and death on the glaciers or when facing one of the fell monsters of the North. Indeed, equipment and treasure are a vital part of every heroic adventure. Just as important are the spells and magic items that are a hero’s stock and trade.

This chapter lists the equipment available to PCs. It also contains magic items PCs might find during their adventures. Finally, it contains a selection of spells commonly seen throughout the Northlands.

Equipment

Equipment is a vital part of every heroic adventure. This section lists the equipment, such as armor, weapons, and gear, available to PCs and explains the rules for using it.

Weapons

This section lists new weapons found in the Northlands. The Weapons table lists the cost, damage dice and type, weight, weapon options, and special properties for weapons. Each weapon has a mastery property. To use a mastery property, you must have a class feature that lets you use it. The table is also split by the proficiencies required (Simple or Martial) to wield the various weapons most effectively.

Masterwork Weapons

A masterwork weapon is an exceptionally well-made version of a weapon of the finest materials available, typically crafted by an expert. While wielding a masterwork weapon you’re proficient with, you have +1 bonus to attack rolls and damage rolls with that weapon (with the bonus damage matching the weapon’s damage type). Note that magic weapons are already considered to be masterwork quality, so masterwork bonuses don’t stack with bonuses already built into magic weapons. You can’t simply make an existing weapon masterwork though. It must be crafted as a masterwork weapon from the start. A masterwork weapon can be purchased for an amount equal to the weapon’s base cost + 300 gp. A single masterwork piece of ammunition costs a flat 5 GP (ignore ammunition base cost).

Atgeir. A polearm with a large, single-edged blade attached along the side of the haft. Atgeirs are thrusting weapons often used to lever enemies out of position after they have been struck.

Bearded Axe. The hook-shaped “beard” at the lower edge of the head of a bearded axe gives it its characteristic silhouette and provides a larger cutting edge than standard battle axes without excessively increasing its weight.

Breidox. Essentially a greataxe with a longer haft, the breidox was developed to deliver a powerful slash to opponents behind a protective shield wall.

Bryntroll. A massive chopping blade mounted at the end of a long pole, the bryntroll is designed to pierce the tough hides of giants.

Seax. These fighting knives are too heavy to be thrown, but they boast long blades that can punch through leather and mail.

Snaerispear. These short, javelin-like spears can be hurled great distances by means of a “throwing string,” a leather strap affixed to the mid-point of the spear shaft. This allows the snaerispear to deal more damage when thrown than in melee.

Mastery Property

Mastery properties are usable only by a character who has a feature, such as Weapon Mastery, that unlocks the property for the character. A new mastery property unique to weapons of the Northlands is defined below.

Pull

If you hit a creature with this weapon, you can pull the creature 5 feet toward yourself if it is Large or smaller. When you do so, you can also move back 5 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks, and this does not use any of your normal movement speed.

Armor

This section lists new types of armor found in the Northlands that are available to PCs, and it covers special rules regarding their use. The Armor table lists the cost, AC bonus, weight, and proficiency required for each kind of armor.

Beinagrind. This armor is formed by carving disks or short shingles out of whale or giant bones and lacing them to a backing of flexible leather.

Double Mail. Also called tvifold brynja, the rings of this chain mail are doubled, so each pair is passed through by four pairs of other rings. It is very heavy and bulky.

Hardened Mail Shirt. This chain shirt has rings of adamantine or mithral woven into key areas to provide more protection than normal mail.

Walrus Hide. These thick, treated walrus hide suits are flexible but quite heavy. In addition to its defensive benefits, walrus hide counts as winter clothing when determining the effects of extreme cold.

Adventuring Gear

This section covers standard nonmagical equipment and the rules for using it.

Jotunskull Helm. Favored by those who wish to project a fearsome aspect, these helms are fashioned from the cleaned bones of a sufficiently large giant. When you wear one, you have Advantage on Charisma (Intimidation) checks against Giants (at the GM’s discretion).

Skis. While wearing skis, you ignore Difficult Terrain from deep snow and move at double your normal Speed while going downhill in snow. You have Disadvantage on Dexterity saves and checks while wearing skis, except those related to moving through snow.

Skates. While wearing skates, you ignore Difficult Terrain from slippery ice and automatically pass Dexterity (Acrobatics) checks to avoid have the Prone condition on ice. You move at double your normal Speed while on ice, but you have a Speed of 5 feet off the ice.

Snow Goggles. These thick glasses are set with lenses of smoky glass, preventing snow blindness.

Snow Visor. These thick bone or wood visors are bound to the head and are constructed with a narrow slit for the wearer’s eyes. Creatures wearing snow visors have Immunity to snow blindness but have Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks relating to sight.

Snowshoes. While wearing snowshoes, you can ignore Difficult Terrain from deep snow. You have Disadvantage on Dexterity saves and checks while wearing snowshoes.

Talharpa. A musical instrument with two to four strings similar to a lyre and played with a bow.

Mounts and Land Vehicles

Travel across the Northlands can be its own adventure. From howling blizzards to roaring giants, just moving from settlement to settlement pits the adventurer against nature and monsters alike. This section details various mounts and land vehicles the intrepid Northlanders use to travel.

Mounts

A mount can help you move more quickly through the wilderness, but it can also carry gear that would otherwise slow you down. The Mounts table shows each animal’s carrying capacity. See Chapter 8 for new mount stat blocks.

Mounts and Cargo. An animal pulling a carriage, cart, chariot, sled, or wagon can move weight up to five times its base carrying capacity, including the weight of the vehicle. If multiple animals pull the same vehicle, add their carrying capacities together.

Barding. Barding is armor designed for a mount. Any type of armor can be purchased as barding. The cost is four times the normal cost, and it weighs twice as much.

Land Vehicles

Land vehicles can travel on solid surfaces and across physical terrain. Much like the longships presented later, the land vehicles in this section have the following statistics.

Speed. The speeds in the table include movement during a combat round, miles per hour, and miles per day.

Crew. A vehicle typically has one driver, but other crew positions may be available. The minimum number of skilled hirelings needed to operate a vehicle depends on the type of vehicle, as shown in the table.

Passengers. The table lists the number of Small and Medium passengers the vehicle can accommodate.

Vehicle Repair. If a vehicle has taken damage and has fewer than half its hit points (rounded down), a creature can spend 1 hour or more and the necessary gp worth of spare parts to repair the damage. At the end of each hour, the vehicle regains Hit Points equal to its AC (up to the vehicle’s Hit Point Maximum). One hour of repairs requires spare parts worth a gp amount equal to 10 percent of the vehicle’s base cost, as shown on the Vehicle table.

Dog Sled

Dog sleds are long, narrow frames set on metal ski-like runners and pulled by teams of trained dogs. Ideal for crossing frozen terrain, dog sleds are commonly used in arctic regions for travel, trade, and transport.

A dog sled is a Large vehicle, weighs 150 pounds, has room on the back for one driver, and can hold 3 passengers or about 800 pounds of cargo. Sleds can be swift, but much depends on the terrain, snow conditions, and other obstacles.

Dog sleds typically have teams of dogs numbering between 6 and 10. For ease of play, use the stats for a wolf for an average sled dog. After 6 hours of travel, a sled dog must take a Long Rest or suffer one level of Exhaustion.

Ogre War Sled

This brutal, oversized war sled is constructed from heavy timber, mammoth bones, and forged metal. The front features a shield-like prow crafted from a mammoth skull that provides Half Cover for the driver. Typically, war sleds are pulled by two worgs, though some are harnessed to a mammoth or other cold weather creatures.

Used by many of the giant and troll communities in the Northlands, the ogre war sled is a Huge vehicle. This heavy sled is large enough to hold two ogres.

Bulky Construction. Creatures that aren’t Large must make a DC 13 Wisdom check (Animal Handling) each turn to control the sled in battle. On a failure, the sled moves its speed in a random direction (other than backward).

Mounted Vantage. Passengers on a moving war sled have a +2 bonus to weapon damage rolls against creatures not also on a mount or vehicle.

Skull Shield. Large creatures riding on the war-sled have Half Cover from ranged attacks.

Actions

On its turn, the driver of the war sled may use one of the following actions in place of its normal actions.

Sled Trample. The sled moves at least 30 feet in a straight line. Each creature trampled by the sled must make a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 2d10 Bludgeoning and 2d10 Slashing damage, and has the Prone condition. On a successful save, the target takes half as much damage and does not have the Prone condition.

Worg Attack. The sled moves at least 10 feet in a straight line, ending adjacent to a creature or object. The two worgs pulling the sled may each make one attack against the target. If both worgs hit with their attacks, the driver may also make one melee weapon attack against the target.

Longships

The signature sea vessel of the Northlands is the iconic longship. Swift and agile in the water, longships are preferred by raiders, reavers, and Northern merchants of all sorts. Longships feature a removeable figurehead mounted on the prow, often carved to resemble a fearsome dragon or other beast, such as a raven or wolf, though some are sculpted as maidens, merfolk, and other aquatic creatures. The stern of the ship sports a carving as well, typically the tail of some fantastic monster.

All longships share a common design, with a keel, a stem at the prow and stern, and a hull. While many other ships have hulls with planks that meet edge-to-edge, a longship’s design overlaps the planks of the hull. This pattern, known as lapstrake or clinker-built, makes longships durable against rough waters. Longship keel designs vary, with some being flatter and able to traverse shallow water and others deeper for carrying more cargo. Longship designs also share rowing benches and oars, as well as a mast for a square sail. Oars allow the crew to quickly move a longship in and out of position, even bringing shallow-keeled ships onto shore. A steering rudder on the starboard side of a longship allows it to be directed easily, whether the ship is moving forward or backward.

Ship Action. At the end of each round, a vehicle with the required number of crew members can move up to its Speed and use one action. The section on Naval Combat in Chapter 7 details rules for fighting ship-to-ship as well as various shipboard actions. The specialized actions listed in the ship stat blocks in this section can be used as shipboard actions.

The longships presented in this section differ from the one found in the core rules.

Drakkar

The largest of longships, a drakkar is also called a “dragon ship” for its stylized dragon figureheads. Drakkar are the flagships of their fleet, and they are commissioned by jarls, chieftains, and other prominent, wealthy leaders.

The larger cargo hold of a drakkar also makes it ideal for long journeys. It is capable of carrying enough supplies to reach destinations that smaller longships would be ill-equipped for. However, drakkar are not as agile as other longships in terms of turning speed or in lighter wind conditions.

Actions

On its turn, the drakkar can take two actions, choosing from the options below (it can take the same action multiple times). It can take only one action if it has fewer than 40 crew. It can’t move or take actions if it has fewer than 3 crew.

Fire Ballista. Ranged Weapon Attack: +8 hit, range 120/480 ft., one target. Hit: 24 (3d10 + 8) Piercing damage.

Shield Wall. The rowers raise their shields in defense. This grants creatures in the ship a +5 bonus to their Armor Class and Dexterity Saving throws, but the ship’s current Speed is halved until the end of the next round.

Karvi

The smallest of the flat-keel longships, the karvi can traverse shallow waters to transport goods, fish, and even raiders. While a karvi can be used in battle, it is often used as a general-purpose river and coastal ship. Its size and shallow keel make it less ocean-worthy than other longboats. While a knarr is used for transporting livestock, it is common to find a karvi ferrying people to their destination.

Knarr

Suited for open water rather than shallow rivers, knarr are longships used to ferry goods and livestock across the ocean. Its deep hull allows for more cargo storage, compared with other longships of similar size, and allows the ship to be operated by smaller crews. When used in exploration or war, knarr are used to carry supplies for the crews and warriors on other ships in the fleet, rather than as battleships.

Fabled Magic Items

Fabled magic items are distinct as a rarity. A fabled item grows in power alongside the character who wields it. Rules for fabled items are summarized here, and a few fabled magic items can be found in this chapter.

A fabled magic item begins as a Common magic item. The item grants a minor, useful trait to its owner that doesn’t require attunement to use.

To access any higher-level trait, a PC must attune to it using standard Attunement rules. Once attuned, a PC can use the fabled item’s more powerful properties as they increase in level. If a PC higher than 1st level attunes to a fabled item, its level-dependent properties, up to the character’s current level, are immediately available.

At the GM’s discretion, an item’s more powerful properties might become available only after the character completes a particular quest or event related to the item’s story rather than immediately upon reaching each property’s required level.

If a PC chooses not to attune to a fabled item, it remains a common magic item, granting only its minor property, regardless of the character’s level.

Unless otherwise noted, properties granted by a fabled item are in effect as long at the character is wearing, carrying, or touching the item.

Spell Descriptions

This section presents descriptions of spells commonly used throughout the Northlands. The spells are listed in alphabetical order.

Spells (A-B)

Aegir’s Breath

Evocation Cantrip (Druid, Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: 120 feet

Components: V, S

Duration: Instantaneous

You blow a cloud of frigid air at a creature or object within range. Make a ranged spell attack against the target. On a hit, the target takes 1d10 Cold damage, or 1d12 if the target is at least partially submerged in water.

Cantrip Upgrade. The spell creates two clouds at level 5, three clouds at level 11, and four clouds at level 17. You can direct the clouds at the same target or at different ones. Make a separate attack roll for each cloud.

Angrboda’s Fury

Level 4 Transmutation (Cleric, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: 30 feet

Components: V, S

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute.

When you cast this spell, you open yourself up to become a conduit of the rage of the giant goddess Angrboda. One creature within range makes a Wisdom saving throw. On a failed save, the target becomes Vulnerable to one type of damage of your choice. If the creature has Immunity to the damage type you selected, it loses its Immunity and takes damage as normal, but it does not have Vulnerability to the damage type you chose.

The creature can repeat the Wisdom saving throw at the end of each of its turns, ending this spell on a success.

Aspect of the Narwhal

Level 2 Transmutation (Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: Touch

Components: V, S

Duration: Concentration, up to 10 minutes

A willing creature that you touch takes on the aspects of a narwhal. It grows flippers and a long horn-like tusk. The target gains Resistance to Cold damage while submerged in water, its Speed becomes 5 feet, and it gains a Swim Speed of 50 feet. The target can take a Bonus Action on any turn in which it moves at least 15 feet directly toward an enemy to make a gore attack that deals Piercing damage equal to 2d6 plus its Strength modifier.

Awaken Ship Guardian

Level 8 Conjuration (Cleric, Druid)

Casting time: Action

Range: 30 feet

Components: V, S

Duration: 24 hours

You summon a protective spirit to live within the hull of a Large or larger ship. While the spell lasts, the ship has the following benefits:

Immunity to Fire damage.

Each time the ship takes damage from a Large or larger creature, the attacking creature makes a Wisdom saving throw, taking 5d6 Psychic damage on a failed save or half as much damage on a successful one.

As a Bonus Action while you are on the ship, you can expend a spell slot, and the ship regains Hit Points equal to three times the slot level plus your spellcasting modifier.

Bearstormer

Level 5 Illusion (Bard, Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: 120 feet

Components: V, S, M (a pinch of bear fur)

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute

You create three phantasmal grizzly bears in a 20-foot Cube within range. Until the spell ends, you can use a Bonus Action to move the Cube up to 40 feet.

When the Cube appears, each enemy in it makes an Intelligence saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 18 (4d8) Psychic damage and has the Frightened condition until the end of its next turn. On a successful save, a creature takes half as much damage only. A creature also makes this save when the spell’s area moves into its space and when a creature enters the spell’s area or ends its turn there. A creature makes this save only once per turn.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. You increase the size of the Cube by 20-feet, the number of phantasmal bears by two, and the damage by 9 (2d8) for each spell slot level above 5th.

Beast of Ragnarok

Level 9 Conjuration (Cleric, Druid, Warlock, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V, S, M (a bezoar formed in a wolf’s stomach)

Duration: 1 minute

The spectral aura of Fenris surrounds you as you draw Ragnarok to the world for a moment. Until the spell ends, the sky grows dark, casting the world in Dim Light in a 1-mile Sphere centered on you. Thick snows fall inside the Sphere, causing the ground to become Difficult Terrain and vision to be Heavily Obscured.

You gain the following benefits for the Duration:

You see clearly within the Sphere and are unaffected by the Difficult Terrain caused by the spell.

You have Advantage on all d20 Tests.

All creatures have Disadvantage on attack rolls against you.

You have Immunity to all types of damage except for one. Roll a d12 to randomly determine a damage type (1. Acid, 2. Bludgeoning, 3. Fire, 4. Force, 5. Lightning, 6. Necrotic, 7. Piercing, 8. Poison, 9. Psychic, 10. Radiant, 11. Slashing, or 12. Thunder.) You have Vulnerability to the damage type you roll.

You have Advantage on saving throws to resist or end all conditions.

Bergelmir’s Provocation

Level 3 Enchantment (Bard, Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard)

Casting time: Reaction, which you take when you see a creature make an attack or spell attack

Range: 60 feet

Components: V

Duration: Instantaneous

The creature making the attack must make a Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, the creature makes the attack against a different eligible target of your choice, which cannot include the creature itself. If there is no other eligible target, the creature’s attack is wasted.

Beseech the Norns

Level 4 Divination (Cleric)

Casting time: Bonus Action

Range: Self

Components: V, S, M (a silver spindle worth 100+ GP, which the spell consumes)

Duration: Special (see text)

You contact one of the three Norns, the weavers of fate, to intercede on your behalf by altering the past, present, or future. Choose one of the following effects:

Change the Past (Uror). Choose a creature you can see that died since the start of your last turn. That creature is returned to life with 1 Hit Point. This spell can’t revive any creature that has died of old age, nor does it restore any missing body parts.

Guide the Present (Verthandi). Treat the first attack roll or ability check you roll before the end of your turn as a natural 20.

Shape the Future (Skuld). Beginning at the start of your next turn and lasting for 1 minute, you have Advantage on all ability checks and saving throws.

If you cast the spell more than once before finishing a Long Rest, there is a cumulative 25 percent chance for each casting after the first that the spell has no effect (other than to consume the material component).

Billowing Sails

Level 2 Evocation (Druid, Ranger, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: 1 minute or Ritual

Range: Touch

Components: V, S

Duration: Concentration, up to 8 hours

You touch a ship or other wind-powered vehicle and fill its sails with a strong breeze. Whenever the ship travels by sail, its Speed increases by 10 ft. or by 1 mph.

Bone-Chilling Smite

Level 3 Necromancy (Paladin)

Casting time: Bonus Action, which you take immediately after hitting a creature with a melee weapon or an Unarmed Strike.

Range: Self

Components: V

Duration: 1 minute

The target takes an extra 3d8 Necrotic damage from the attack, and it must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or gain one level of Exhaustion until the spell ends.

At the end of each of its turns, the Exhausted target repeats the save. On a success, it removes all Exhaustion caused by this spell. On a failure, it receives one level of Exhaustion.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. The damage increases by 1d8 for each spell slot level above 3rd.

Bound Fortunes

Level 5 Necromancy (Cleric)

Casting time: Action

Range: Touch

Components: V, S, M (two small spindles bound together with thread)

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 hour

You touch another willing creature and bind your fates together until the spell ends. For the duration, if the target is within 60 feet of you and makes an ability check, attack roll, or saving throw, you may use your Reaction to allow the target to reroll and use the new result. The target can do the same to you. Each time you do this, your, and only your Hit Point maximum is reduced by 1d4, and the same is true of the target. If this reduces your Hit Point maximum to 0, you die. This reduction is removed when the spell ends.

Bragi’s Theatrical Fall

Level 5 Illusion (Bard, Cleric, Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard)

Casting time: Reaction, which you take when an attack would reduce you to 0 Hit Points but not kill you outright

Range: Self

Components: V, M (a drop of your blood)

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 hour

With a gasp and loud proclamation, you seem to fall dead to the ground. Instead of being reduced to 0 Hit Points, you are reduced to a number of Hit points equal to your spellcasting ability modifier. At the same time, you have the Invisible condition, and an illusion of your dead body appears, lying in the space where your body would have fallen.

If a creature takes the Study action to examine your illusory body, the creature can determine it is an illusion with a successful Intelligence (Investigation) check against your spell save DC. If a creature discerns the illusion for what it is, the illusion becomes faint to the creature.

The Invisible condition ends if you take the Attack action, use the Magic action to cast a spell that lets a creature regain Hit Points, or use any action to cause harm to a creature.

Branch and Root of Yggdrasil

Level 5 Conjuration (Druid)

Casting time: Action or Ritual

Range: Touch

Components: V, S, M (branch, leaf, or bark from a World Tree)

Duration: Instantaneous

All trees are intrinsically linked to Yggdrasil. When you cast this spell and touch a tree, you and up to eight willing creatures in contact with that tree are transported to a random location on Yggdrasil. If you are touching a World Tree found on the mortal plane (such as those found in the Northlands) when you cast the spell, you can specify a target destination on Yggdrasil in general terms, and you appear in or near that destination, as determined by the GM.

Using this magic requires a sacrifice from the caster. After casting this spell, your Hit Point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to a roll of one of your Hit Dice until you complete a Long Rest.

Spells (C-E)

Call to Action

Level 2 Enchantment (Bard, Paladin)

Casting time: Reaction, which you take in response to another creature’s turn starting

Range: 60 feet

Components: V

Duration: Instantaneous

The creature starting its turn may immediately use its Reaction to take the Dash, Disengage, Dodge, or Help action.

Claws of the Bear

Level 4 Transmutation (Cleric, Druid, Ranger)

Casting time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V, S, M (a tuft of bear fur)

Duration: Concentration, up to 10 minutes

You imbue yourself with the claws of a bear. You transform your arms into a pair of muscular, furred paws and make a melee spell attack against a target. On a hit, the target takes Slashing damage equal to 3d10 plus your spellcasting ability modifier and, if the target is no more than one size category larger than you, it has the Prone condition. For the duration of the spell, you can make attacks with the claws.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. When you cast this spell using a 5th- or 6th-level spell slot, the damage increases to 4d10. When you cast it using a 7th- or 8th-level spell slot, the damage increases to 5d10. When you cast it using a spell slot of 9th level, the damage increases to 6d8.

Coldheart

Level 1 Necromancy (Druid)

Casting time: Action

Range: 30 feet

Components: V, S

Duration: Instantaneous

You cause a supernatural chill to fall upon one Beast or Humanoid that you can see within range. The target must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failed save, it receives 1 Exhaustion level.

At the end of its next turn, the target must make another Constitution saving throw. On a failed save, the creature receives 1 Exhaustion level. Then the spell ends. Exhaustion applied by this spell is not removed by the end of the spell.

Compel Avarice

Level 3 Enchantment (Cleric, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: 30 feet

Components: V, S, M (a gold coin)

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute

This spell twists the target creature’s perceptions, inflaming its greed. You choose an object you can see when you cast this spell. The target must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or be compelled to try to take that item for itself for the duration of the spell. It will try to steal the item if possible, and if it is unable to, it will fight the item’s current holder. If the target is able to claim the object, it will flee from all other creatures to jealously guard it until the spell ends. Creatures that have Immunity to the Charmed condition automatically succeed on their saving throws against this spell.

Disrupt the Wyrd

Level 2 Abjuration (Cleric)

Casting time: Reaction, which you take in response to a saving throw being rolled by a creature that you can see within 30 feet of yourself

Range: 30 feet

Components: V, S, M (a tangle of thread)

Duration: Instantaneous

The creature rerolls the saving throw, either adding your spellcasting modifier to or subtracting it from the new roll. It must use the new result.

Drummer’s Cadence

Level 1 Enchantment (Bard)

Casting time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V

Duration: Concentration, up to 8 hours

For the duration, you radiate an aura in a 60-foot Emanation. Whenever an allied ship in this area moves by oar, its Speed increases by 10 ft. The vessel must be sufficiently crewed for this spell to have any effect.

Earthsail

Level 4 Transmutation (Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: 1 minute or Ritual

Range: Touch

Components: V, S, M (a handful of mud and a handful of sea water)

Duration: 8 hours

You touch a sailing vessel with a crew of up to 10 throughout the spell’s casting, imbuing it with the ability to treat earth as though it were sea. For the duration, the vessel treats soil, mud, and packed dirt as though it were water, pushing through it and leaving a rippling wake of re-solidified terrain. Snow, ice, plants (including trees), and small stones are temporarily swept aside in the ship’s wake, but boulders and mountains must be sailed around.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. If you cast this spell with a 7th-level spell slot, it can affect a vessel with a crew of up to 30, and it lasts for 24 hours. If you cast this spell with a 9th-level spell slot, it can affect a vessel with a crew of any size, and it lasts for 1 week.

Encase in Ice

Level 2 Conjuration (Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: 60 feet

Components: V, S, M (a handful of water)

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute

A crashing wave of water fills a 20-foot square on ground that you can see within range. The water instantly freezes in place. Each creature in the area makes a Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, a creature’s legs are trapped in the ice, and the creature has the Restrained condition until the spell ends. A creature with the Restrained condition can use an action to make a Strength (Athletics) check against your spell save DC, ending the condition on itself on a success. Another creature can take the Help action to attempt to free the trapped creature, making the same check.

Creatures that were not in the spell’s area when you cast it treat the area as Difficult Terrain. If a creature that is not Restrained by the ice enters the area or ends its turn there, it must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw or have the Prone condition.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. If you cast this spell with a 3rd-level spell slot, the ice entraps the target’s arms as well as its legs. The target cannot take the Attack action or cast spells with somatic components until the Restrained condition ends.

If you cast this spell using a 5th-level spell slot, the ice completely entombs creatures in the area when you cast the spell. Creatures that fail their initial save are Restrained and have Total Cover for the spell’s duration. An affected creature can make a Strength save at the end of each of its turns to break out, but whether it succeeds or fails, it takes 2d6 Cold damage as its flesh, which was stuck to the ice, tears.

Ensnared Threads

Level 6 Divination (Cleric, Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: 60 feet

Components: V, S, M (a knotted string of wool)

Duration: 1 minute

The Norns allow you to briefly force a connection between the threads of your fate and those of another creature. A creature you can see within range must make a Wisdom saving throw. On a failed save, you form a connection between yourself and the target.

Once per turn, until the spell ends, the first attack that hits you hits the target creature instead, if it can be targeted by the attack. If that creature cannot be the target of the attack, the attack hits you normally.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. If you cast this spell with an 8th- or 9th- level spell slot, all attacks that hit you instead hit the target. Any of the attacks the creature cannot be the target of still hit you normally.

Extinguish

Level 1 Transmutation (Druid, Ranger, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V

Duration: Instantaneous

You extinguish all magical and nonmagical flames of your choice in a 60-foot Cube originating from you. Creatures made partially or completely of fire take 1d12 Force damage.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. For each spell slot above 1st that you use to cast this spell, the cube increases by 10 feet and affected creatures take an additional 1d12 Force damage.

Eyes of the Raven

Level 2 Transmutation (Druid)

Casting time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V, S, M (a raven feather)

Duration: Concentration, up to 10 minutes

You call upon the power of Wotan’s all-knowing ravens, Huginn and Muninn. While the spell lasts, you have Advantage on Intelligence checks and Intelligence saves.

As a Magic action, you can gain Truesight with a range of 30 feet. This lasts until the start of your next turn. Then the spell ends.

Fenris’s Howl

Level 5 Necromancy (Ranger)

Casting time: Action

Range: 120 feet

Components: V, S

Duration: 1 minute

You channel the piercing, death-dirge howl of the Fenris wolf at a point that you can see within range and gain strength from others’ fear. Creatures that you choose within 60 feet of that point that can hear you must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or have the Frightened condition (with you as the source) for the duration of the spell. While at least one creature affected by the spell remains Frightened, your Speed is doubled, and you deal an extra 3d6 Necrotic damage to a Frightened enemy with each of your attacks.

Fist of the Frost Jarl

Level 4 Evocation (Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V, S, M (a drop of water melted from an icicle)

Duration: Special

A fist of solid ice filling a 5-foot Cube hurtles from you in a direction you choose in a 120-foot Line. Each creature in the Line makes a Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 18 (4d8) Cold damage. On a successful save, a creature takes half damage.

You may choose for one creature of Huge size or smaller that fails its save to have the Grappled condition, which has an escape DC equal to your spell save DC. A creature that ends its turn grappled by the fist takes 9 (2d8) Cold damage. When a creature escapes the grapple, the fist shatters.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. The damage increases by 1d8 for each spell slot level above 4th. If cast using a spell slot of 7th level or higher, the spell ignores Resistance to Cold damage.

Freezing Fog

Level 3 Transmutation (Druid)

Casting time: Action

Range: 120 feet

Components: V, S

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 hour

You create a 20-foot-radius Sphere of bone-chilling fog centered on a point within range. The Sphere is Heavily Obscured and considered Difficult Terrain. The fog lingers in the air for the duration or until a strong wind (such as one created by Gust of Wind) disperses it.

Each creature that starts its turn in the Sphere, or moves into its area, must succeed on a Constitution saving throw. On a failed save, a target’s Speed is halved until the end of its next turn.

When a creature moves into or within the area, it takes 2d4 Cold damage for every 5 feet it travels.

You have Immunity to all of the effects of this spell.

Freyja’s Allure

Level 1 Enchantment (Bard, Cleric, Sorcerer, Warlock)

Casting time: Action

Range: 30 feet

Components: V

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute

One creature of your choice that you can see within range makes a Charisma saving throw. On a failed save, the target has Disadvantage on attack rolls and can’t willingly move farther away from you for the duration.

At the end of each of its turns, the creature can make another Charisma saving throw. The target has Disadvantage on the save if it can see you and you are within 30 feet of it. On a successful save, the spell ends on it.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. You can target one additional creature for each spell slot level above 1st.

Freyja’s Grace

Level 1 Abjuration (Bard, Paladin, Sorcerer, Warlock)

Casting time: Reaction, which you take after failing a saving throw.

Range: Self

Components: V

Duration: Instantaneous

Reroll the saving throw as a Charisma save, regardless of the original save type.

Ghostly Crew

Level 4 Necromancy (Cleric, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: Touch

Components: V, S

Duration: Concentration, up to 8 hours

You summon intangible spirits to crew a touched vessel. For the duration of the spell, as long as you are within 30 feet of the vessel, it is considered to have a sufficient number of sailors to take a full complement of normal ship actions (including movement and using siege weapons). The spirits can’t take other actions or engage in any other activities, such as fighting independently or repairing the vessel.

Giantdodge

Level 2 Abjuration (Ranger)

Casting time: Bonus Action

Range: Self

Components: V

Duration: 1 minute

Increase your Speed by 20 feet. You gain a +2 Bonus to your AC against attacks made by Large or larger creatures, and you do not provoke Opportunity attacks from them.

Giant’s Teeth

Level 3 Conjuration (Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action

Range: 90 feet

Components: V, S, M (a handful of molars)

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute

Two half-circles of enormous teeth erupt from the ground and slam together to crush a creature of your choice within 90 feet.

The target has the Grappled condition. The escape DC is equal to your spell save DC. At the beginning of each of its turns while it has the Grappled condition, the creature takes 2d10 Bludgeoning damage. If the target escapes, the spell ends.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. When you cast this spell using a 5th-level spell slot, the damage increases to 3d10. When you cast it using a 7th-level spell slot, the damage increases to 5d8. When you cast it using a spell slot of 9th level, the damage increases to 6d8.

Spells (H-N)

Hearthfire

Level 4 Abjuration (Cleric, Ranger, Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting time: Action or Ritual

Range: Touch

Components: V, M (a bit of charcoal)

Duration: 8 hours

You touch one Large or smaller object that isn’t being worn or carried by someone else. Until the spell ends, the object sheds Bright Light in a 20-foot radius and Dim Light for an additional 20 feet. Within the spell’s area of Bright Light, you and each creature you choose have Immunity to the harmful effects of Extreme Cold.

Hungry Jaws of Fenris

Level 6 Transmutation (Druid)

Casting time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V, S, M

Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute

As a Magic action, you channel the power of Fenris, the Great Wolf, transforming your mouth into a gaping maw. This doesn’t impede your movement or actions, but you can’t speak or cast spells with verbal components for the duration.

While the spell is active, you can use your jaws to make a bite attack. On a hit, the target takes Piercing damage equal to 4d10 plus your spellcasting ability modifier, and it has the Grappled condition (escape DC equals your spell save DC).

If you bite a target already Grappled by you, you can choose to swallow it, and the Grappled condition ends. A swallowed creature is shunted into a pocket dimension. While there, it has the Blinded and Restrained conditions, has Total Cover against attacks and other effects outside the dimension, and takes 19 (3d12) Force damage at the start of each of your turns. You can’t swallow a creature if you have already swallowed a creature using this spell.

Concentration
Player’s Handbook
Rule

Some spells and other effects require Concentration to remain active, as specified in their descriptions. If the effect's creator loses Concentration, the effect ends. If the effect has a maximum duration, the effect's description specifies how long the creator can concentrate on it: up to 1 minute, 1 hour, or some other duration. The creator can end Concentration at any time (no action required). The following factors break Concentration.

Another Concentration Effect. You lose Concentration on an effect the moment you start casting a spell that requires Concentration or activate another effect that requires Concentration.

Damage. If you take damage, you must succeed on a Constitution saving throw to maintain Concentration. The DC equals 10 or half the damage taken (round down), whichever number is higher, up to a maximum DC of 30.

Incapacitated or Dead. Your Concentration ends if you have the Incapacitated condition or you die.
